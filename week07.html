The writing principle

Meaning first → structure second

A longer sentence is not automatically more academic. Choose the relationship that the evidence supports, then punctuate it accurately.

Bridge from Week 6 · 0–5 minutes

0 · Diagnose the evidence before you join sentences

This is a condensed 60-minute session; the regular course timetable is 120 minutes including a rest.

Academic sentences communicate evidence and reasoning, not just grammar. This opening task uses Week 6's illustrative PV data and reminds students why a plausible explanation is not automatically a demonstrated cause.

Simulation at 14:004.0 kW predicted output.
Measurement at 14:002.8 kW measured mean.
Missing evidenceIrradiance was not recorded.
0 · Five-minute diagnostic warm-up (0–5 min)

Draft A: At 14:00, the simulation predicted 4.0 kW. The measured mean was 2.8 kW. Cloud cover definitely caused this difference.

Draft B: At 14:00, the simulation predicted 4.0 kW, but the measured mean was 2.8 kW. Because irradiance was not recorded, the cause could not be established from these data.

Task: 2 min alone: choose the more defensible draft and mark the linking words. 2 min pairs: explain why. 1 min lecturer debrief.

Three checks to apply all lesson

1 · What is known?Report values, units and observation.
2 · What is the link?Contrast, condition, time or a justified reason.
3 · Is the claim fair?Do not add a cause or significance that was not tested.
Transition: A writer may express the same two observations in two sentences, a compound sentence, or a complex sentence. The choice is not decoration: it tells the reader which idea matters most (page 5).

What success looks like

  • Identify the two reported numbers and their units.
  • Underline the linking word (but or because) and explain the relationship.
  • Identify why cloud cover cannot be presented as the established cause.

All data are illustrative classroom values from Week 6, not published experimental findings.

Explain · 5–15 minutes

1 · Build with clauses

An independent clause has a subject and a finite verb and can stand alone. A dependent clause has a subject and verb but cannot be a complete sentence by itself.

Independent clause

The measured output decreased.

Subject = output; verb = decreased. Complete meaning.

Dependent clause

Although the measured output decreased …

Subject + verb are present, but although leaves an unfinished meaning.

Four sentence types: count the clauses

TypeClause count (I = independent, D = dependent)Engineering example
Simple (S)1 IThe converter supplied the test load.
Compound (CP)2 or more I, joined by , and / , but / , so or a semicolonThe simulation predicted 4.0 kW at 14:00, but the measured mean was 2.8 kW.
Complex (CX)1 I + at least 1 D (D before or after the main clause)Although the simulation predicted 4.0 kW, the measured mean at 14:00 was 2.8 kW.
Compound-complex (CC)2 or more I + at least 1 DAlthough only one prototype of each design was tested, Design B was more efficient at 80% load, and it remained more efficient at 100% load.
Length is not type. “The prototype operated smoothly during the short laboratory test” is simple: one subject–verb core. Two verbs sharing one subject (“The efficiency rose and then fell”) are still one clause.

Activity A · Label the type (4 minutes)

A1

The inverter supplied the load.

A2

The voltage remained stable, but the current increased.

A3

When the load changed, the output voltage fluctuated.

A4

Because the temperature exceeded the threshold.

A5

When the load reached 100%, Design B remained more efficient, but its margin was only 1.2 percentage points.

A6

Design A was more efficient at 10% load and less efficient at 80% load.

Write S, CP, CX, CC or fragment beside each item. For A5, bracket every clause and mark it I or D.

Check Activity A (website self-study)

A1 = S. A2 = CP. A3 = CX. A4 = fragment: a because clause needs a main clause. A5 = CC: [When the load reached 100%] D + [Design B remained more efficient] I + [its margin was only 1.2 percentage points] I. A6 = S: one subject (Design A) with two linked complements; and does not create a second clause here.

2 · Coordinate equal ideas

Coordination gives two complete clauses equal weight: choose it when the reader should remember both. The seven coordinators are and, but, or, nor, yet, so, for; in technical writing, and (addition), but (contrast) and so (a justified result) do most of the work. The standard pattern is clause, conjunction clause.

ChoiceMeaningEngineering model
andTwo factsThe output was recorded, and the data were stored for analysis.
butContrastDesign A was more efficient at 10% load, but Design B was more efficient at 100% load.
soSupported resultIrradiance was not recorded, so the cause of the PV output difference could not be established from these data.

Punctuation that changes readability

Correct

The simulation predicted 4.0 kW, but the measurement was 2.8 kW.

The simulation predicted 4.0 kW; however, the measurement was 2.8 kW.

Incorrect

The simulation predicted 4.0 kW, the measurement was 2.8 kW.

The simulation predicted 4.0 kW, however, the measurement was 2.8 kW.

Scientific logic check: so asserts a result relation. A writer cannot insert it merely because two measurements happen in sequence. If a cause is untested, report the association instead.

Activity B · Make the meaning clear (5 minutes)

B1 · Join with “but”

Design A was more efficient at 10% load. Design B was more efficient at 100% load.

B2 · Use “however” with correct punctuation

At 14:00, simulation predicted 4.0 kW. The measured mean was 2.8 kW.

B3 · Audit the claim

The measured PV output was lower, so cloud cover caused the reduction.

Rewrite without claiming an unmeasured cause.

Check Activity B (website self-study)

B1: Design A was more efficient at 10% load, but Design B was more efficient at 100% load. B2: At 14:00, the simulation predicted 4.0 kW; however, the measured mean was 2.8 kW. B3: The measured mean was below the prediction at 14:00, but the cause could not be established because irradiance was not recorded.

3 · Subordinate a supporting idea

Use a dependent clause to express reason, contrast, time or condition. First identify the relationship; then choose the correct word. The idea in the main clause is the one the reader remembers.

WordRelationshipAccurate engineering model
becauseEvidence-supported reasonBecause only one prototype was tested, the findings cannot establish between-unit variability.
althoughConcession / contrastAlthough Design B reached 96.7% efficiency at 80% load, only one prototype of that design was tested.
whenTime / observed conditionWhen the load reached 100%, Design B's efficiency was 96.3%.
ifCondition / possibilityIf additional prototypes are tested, the estimate of variability may change.
whileContrast or simultaneity (in theses, prefer whereas for contrast)Design A reached 91.0% at 10% load, while Design B reached 86.5%.
Commas: clause first → comma: “Although only one prototype was tested, Design B reached 96.7%.” Clause last → comma before contrast words (although, whereas, while): “Design B reached 96.7%, although only one prototype was tested.” No comma before an essential because / when / if clause.

Why choose coordination or subordination? Emphasis

Writer's choiceSame two factsWhat the reader takes away
Coordinate (equal weight)Design B was more efficient at 100% load, but only one prototype was tested.Two points of equal importance
Subordinate the limitationAlthough only one prototype was tested, Design B was more efficient at 100% load.The result: suits a Results paragraph
Subordinate the resultAlthough Design B was more efficient at 100% load, only one prototype was tested.The limitation: suits a Limitations paragraph

Activity C · Choose the connector and the emphasis (4 minutes)

C1

_____ only one prototype was tested, the results should be interpreted cautiously. (Because / Although)

C2

_____ Design B showed higher efficiency at 100% load, the test does not show its performance under all operating conditions. (Although / Because)

C3 · Make the limitation the main point

Design A was more efficient at 10% load, but only one prototype of each design was tested.

Check Activity C (website self-study)

C1 = Because (the single prototype is the reason for caution). C2 = Although (concession: the result is real but limited). C3 = Although Design A was more efficient at 10% load, only one prototype of each design was tested. The limitation is now in the main clause, so it carries the emphasis.

Guided clinic · 33–41 minutes

4 · Repair technical sentences

First identify the problem, then rewrite without changing the technical evidence. Parallel structure means using matching verb forms in a list. Work in pairs and compare solutions.

ProblemIncorrect draftRevision rule
Double connectorAlthough efficiency rose, but uncertainty remained.Remove but. Arabic transfer: also avoid because … therefore.
Comma spliceThe current rose, the voltage stayed constant.Use , and / ; / a full stop.
FragmentBecause only one prototype was tested.Add a complete independent clause.
Misused howeverThe system was stable however the test was short.Use ; however, or separate sentences.
Broken parallelismThe test measured voltage, recording current, and calculated power.Keep equal verbs in one list: measured, recorded, calculated.

Activity D · Four repairs (4 minutes)

D1

Although efficiency rose, but uncertainty remained.

D2

Design A was more efficient at low load, Design B was more efficient at high load.

D3

Because only one prototype of each design was tested.

D4

The test measured voltage, recording current, and calculated input power.

Activity E · Arabic → English (2 minutes)

Translate with although — do not add but.

على الرغم من أن كفاءة التصميم ب بلغت 96.7٪ عند حمل 80٪، فقد اختُبر نموذج أولي واحد فقط من هذا التصميم.

Check Activities D and E (website self-study)

D1: Although efficiency rose, uncertainty remained. D2: Design A was more efficient at low load, but Design B was more efficient at high load. D3: Because only one prototype of each design was tested, generalisation is limited. D4: The test measured voltage, recorded current, and calculated input power. E: Although Design B reached 96.7% efficiency at 80% load, only one prototype of this design was tested.

5 · Before and after: precise, concise, fair

Data: Week 6's illustrative two-converter efficiencies, one prototype of each design, no repeat measurements and no loss breakdown.

Load (% rated)Design A efficiencyDesign B efficiencyB − A (percentage points)
10%91.0%86.5%−4.5
80%96.0%96.7%+0.7
100%95.1%96.3%+1.2

Before · first draft (64 words)

1It can be clearly seen from Table 1 that Design B is 2significantly better than Design A. 3Design B had 96.7% at 80% load. Design A had 96.0%. At 100% load Design B was also higher. 4This proves that the new topology of Design B reduces the losses. 5Design A was better at light load due to the fact that its efficiency was 91.0%.

After · revision (63 words)

CX Design B was more efficient than Design A at 80% and 100% load (by 0.7 and 1.2 percentage points), whereas Design A was more efficient at 10% load (91.0% vs 86.5%). S The advantage of Design B therefore depends on load. CX Because only one prototype of each design was tested and losses were not measured, these data cannot show why Design B performed better.

#Problem in the draftRevision principle
1Wordy opener: “It can be clearly seen from Table 1 that”Concise: state the finding; the table is already cited.
2“significantly better”: no statistical test; better in what?Precise: name the property and the size (0.7 and 1.2 percentage points). Keep significant for a reported statistical test.
3Three short sentences that list numbers with no stated relationshipStructure: one complex sentence; whereas shows the contrast, and the main clause holds the main finding.
4“proves … reduces the losses”: losses were never measuredFair claim: state what the data cannot show.
5“due to the fact that its efficiency was 91.0%”: wordy and circularLogic: a value is not a reason. Keep because for a real reason (here, the reason for caution).

Same length, more information: concision means no wasted words, not fewer facts.

Activity F · Make it precise and concise (1 minute)

It is clearly evident that the proposed controller significantly improves performance due to the fact that overshoot fell from 12% to 8% in one step-response test.

Check Activity F (website self-study)

In one step-response test, the proposed controller reduced overshoot from 12% to 8%. (Delete the empty opener and significantly; name the measured quantity; keep the one-test limit visible.)
