Building Better Academic Sentences

A practical 60-minute lesson in clause control, sentence variety, precise logical links and punctuation for MSc electrical-engineering writing.

The writing principle

Meaning first → structure second

A longer sentence is not automatically more academic. Choose the relationship that the evidence supports, then punctuate it accurately.

Connection to Week 6

Week 6 taught us to observe → compare → interpret → qualify technical data. Week 7 asks how those ideas become complete, varied, readable sentences. Bring your Results and Discussion paragraph, claim audit and revision log to revise their sentence structure.

Bridge from Week 6 · 0–6 minutes

0 · Diagnose the evidence before you join sentences

This is a condensed 60-minute session; the regular course timetable is 120 minutes including a rest.

Academic sentences communicate evidence and reasoning, not just grammar. This opening task uses Week 6's illustrative PV data and reminds students why a plausible explanation is not automatically a demonstrated cause.

Simulation at 14:004.0 kW predicted output.
Measurement at 14:002.8 kW measured mean.
Missing evidenceIrradiance was not recorded.
0 · Six-minute diagnostic warm-up (0–6 min)

Draft A: At 14:00, the simulation predicted 4.0 kW. The measured mean was 2.8 kW. Cloud cover definitely caused this difference.

Draft B: At 14:00, the simulation predicted 4.0 kW, but the measured mean was 2.8 kW. Because irradiance was not recorded, the cause could not be established from these data.

Task: 2 min alone: choose the more defensible draft and mark the linking words. 2 min pairs: explain why. 2 min lecturer debrief.

Three checks to apply all lesson

1 · What is known?Report values, units and observation.
2 · What is the link?Contrast, condition, time or a justified reason.
3 · Is the claim fair?Do not add a cause or significance that was not tested.
Transition: A writer may express the same two observations in two sentences, a compound sentence, or a complex sentence. Which arrangement is clearer depends on what needs emphasis.

What success looks like

  • Identify the two reported numbers and their units.
  • Underline the linking word (but or because) and explain the relationship.
  • Identify why cloud cover cannot be presented as the established cause.

All data are illustrative classroom values from Week 6, not published experimental findings.

Explain · 6–16 minutes

1 · Build with clauses

An independent clause has a subject and a finite verb and can stand alone. A dependent clause has a subject and verb but cannot be a complete sentence by itself.

Independent clause

The measured output decreased.

Subject = output; verb = decreased. Complete meaning.

Dependent clause

Although the measured output decreased …

Subject + verb are present, but although leaves an unfinished meaning.

Three sentence patterns

PatternStructureEngineering example
Simple (S)One independent clauseThe converter supplied the test load.
Compound (CP)Independent + , but + independent (or use a semicolon)The simulation predicted 4.0 kW at 14:00, but the measured mean was 2.8 kW.
Complex (CX)Contains ≥1 dependent clause (before or after the main clause)Although the simulation predicted 4.0 kW, the measured mean was 2.8 kW at 14:00.
Compound-complexCompound + dependentAlthough only one prototype was tested, Design B led at 80% load, and it led at 100%.
Do not confuse sentence length with sentence type. “The prototype operated smoothly during the short laboratory test” is a simple sentence because it has one independent clause, even though it contains many words.

Activity A · Label the pattern (3 minutes)

A1

The inverter supplied the load.   S / CP / CX

A2

The voltage remained stable, but the current increased.   S / CP / CX

A3

When the load changed, the output voltage fluctuated.   S / CP / CX

A4

Because the temperature exceeded the threshold.   Complete / fragment

Thesis tip: Vary sentence structure to emphasise findings, not to increase length.
Check Activity A (website self-study)

A1 = S; A2 = CP; A3 = CX; A4 = fragment. A clause beginning with because needs a complete main clause.

Explain + practice · 16–26 minutes

2 · Coordinate equal ideas

Use and (addition), but (contrast), or so (a justified result; also or, nor, yet) to connect two complete clauses. The standard pattern is clause, conjunction clause.

ChoiceMeaningEngineering model
andTwo factsThe output was recorded, and the data were stored for analysis.
butContrastDesign A was more efficient at 10% load, but Design B was more efficient at 100% load.
soSupported resultIrradiance was not recorded, so the cause of the PV output difference could not be established from these data.

Punctuation that changes readability

Correct

The simulation predicted 4.0 kW, but the measurement was 2.8 kW.

The simulation predicted 4.0 kW; however, the measurement was 2.8 kW.

Incorrect

The simulation predicted 4.0 kW, the measurement was 2.8 kW.

The simulation predicted 4.0 kW, however, the measurement was 2.8 kW.

Scientific logic check: so asserts a result relation. A writer cannot insert it merely because two measurements happen in sequence. If a cause is untested, report the association instead.

Activity B · Make the meaning clear (5 minutes)

B1 · Join with “but”

Design A was more efficient at 10% load. Design B was more efficient at 100% load.

B2 · Use “however” with correct punctuation

At 14:00, simulation predicted 4.0 kW. The measured mean was 2.8 kW.

B3 · Audit the claim

The measured PV output was lower, so cloud cover caused the reduction.

Rewrite without claiming an unmeasured cause.

Check Activity B (website self-study)

B1: Design A was more efficient at 10% load, but Design B was more efficient at 100% load. B2: At 14:00, the simulation predicted 4.0 kW; however, the measured mean was 2.8 kW. B3: The measured mean was below the prediction at 14:00, but the cause could not be established because irradiance was not recorded.

Explain + practice · 26–38 minutes

3 · Subordinate a supporting idea

Use a dependent clause to express reason, contrast, time or condition. First identify the relationship; then choose the correct word.

WordRelationshipAccurate engineering model
becauseEvidence-supported reasonBecause only one prototype was tested, the findings cannot establish between-unit variability.
althoughConcession / contrastAlthough Design B reached 96.7% efficiency at 80% load, only one prototype of that design was tested.
whenTime / observed conditionWhen the load reached 100%, Design B's efficiency was 96.3%.
ifCondition / possibilityIf additional prototypes are tested, the estimate of variability may change.
whileContrast or simultaneity (in theses, prefer whereas for contrast)Design A reached 91.0% at 10% load, while Design B reached 86.5%.

Clause first

Although only one prototype was tested, Design B reached 96.7% efficiency at 80% load.

Use a comma after the introductory dependent clause.

Clause last

Design B reached 96.7% efficiency at 80% load, although only one prototype was tested.

Use a comma before although/whereas/while (contrast); omit it before because/when/if when the clause is essential.

Common Arabic–English transfer errors: Avoid “although … but” and “because … therefore” as double connectors within the same clause pair. Choose one grammatical linking structure.

Activity C · Pick the honest connector (4 minutes)

C1

_____ only one prototype was tested, the results should be interpreted cautiously. (Because / Although)

C2

_____ Design B showed higher efficiency at 100% load, the test does not show its performance under all operating conditions. (Although / Because)

C3

_____ the load reached 80%, Design B's efficiency was 96.7%. (When / But)

Check Activity C (website self-study)

C1 = Because (a reason for caution); C2 = Although (contrast); C3 = When (condition at a recorded load). The connector must preserve the scientific meaning.

Guided clinic · 38–48 minutes

4 · Repair technical sentences

First identify the problem, then rewrite without changing the technical evidence. Parallel structure means using matching verb forms in a list. Work in pairs and compare solutions.

ProblemIncorrect draftRevision rule
Double connectorAlthough efficiency rose, but uncertainty remained.Remove but.
Comma spliceThe current rose, the voltage stayed constant.Use , and / ; / a full stop.
FragmentBecause only one prototype was tested.Add a complete independent clause.
Misused howeverThe system was stable however the test was short.Use ; however, or separate sentences.
Broken parallelismThe test measured voltage, recording current, and calculated power.Keep equal verbs in one list: measured, recorded, calculated.

Activity D · Four repairs (4 minutes)

D1

Although efficiency rose, but uncertainty remained.

D2

Design A was more efficient at low load, Design B was more efficient at high load.

D3

Because only one prototype of each design was tested.

D4

The test measured voltage, recording current, and calculated input power.

Activity E · Arabic → English (2 minutes)

Translate with although — do not add but.

على الرغم من أن كفاءة التصميم ب بلغت 96.7٪ عند حمل 80٪، فقد اختُبر نموذج أولي واحد فقط من هذا التصميم.

Check Activities D and E (website self-study)

D1: Although efficiency rose, uncertainty remained. D2: Design A was more efficient at low load, but Design B was more efficient at high load. D3: Because only one prototype of each design was tested, generalisation is limited. D4: The test measured voltage, recorded current, and calculated input power. 5 · From figures to a short paragraph

Context: Reuse Week 6's illustrative two-converter efficiency data. These values describe one prototype of each design; no repeat measurements are given. At 80% load, the difference is 0.7 percentage points (96.7% − 96.0%).

Load (% rated)Design A efficiencyDesign B efficiency
10%91.0%86.5%
80%96.0%96.7%
100%95.1%96.3%
Independent task · 7 minutes

Write 60–90 words

Use at least one simple, one compound and one complex sentence. Include a quantified comparison, a cautious interpretation and a specific limitation. Do not invent a cause or a statistical significance result.

Optional starters: “At 10% load, …” · “Design A …, but Design B …” · “Although only one prototype …, …”

Peer-check · 2 minutes

1 · GrammarMark S, CP, CX. Does every dependent clause attach to a main clause?
2 · EvidenceCheck values, units, contrast and the limitation against the table.
3 · ConnectorsUnderline and / but / because / although / when / while / if.
4 · RevisionReplace one awkward long sentence with a clearer structure. Compare with your Week 6 paragraph and revise one sentence.
Sentence-quality test: Would the sentence still be scientifically fair if the reader looked at the figure? Grammar should never make the evidence sound stronger than it is.

Sentence surgery: a 4-step checklist

1 · CompleteDoes every sentence have an independent clause?
2 · ConnectDoes the connector mean addition, contrast, reason or condition?
3 · PunctuateCheck comma + but; semicolon + however; fronted dependent clause + comma.
4 · VerifyCheck every quantity and qualify uncertain explanations.
