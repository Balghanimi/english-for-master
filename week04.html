Basis for Week 4: the course document Descriptive Language in Academic Writing for Engineers. In that handout, descriptive writing is presented as writing that provides facts or information without arguing or interpreting. It is especially important when engineers describe materials, methods, results, figures and tables. The original handout was designed as a 45-minute session; this course version expands the practice for our normal class while keeping graph language brief because Weeks 5–6 cover graphs in depth.
Where you use it

Descriptive Writing in Engineering

Descriptive language is not a separate “type of English” — it is a practical function used throughout technical writing.

Methods

Describe what was prepared, measured, tested, simulated or calculated.

Results

Report values, differences, changes and observations before interpreting them.

Figures & Tables

Identify visible patterns, quantities, peaks, declines and comparisons.

Designs & Materials

Describe size, structure, properties, operating conditions and performance.

The target is clearer technical description, not simply longer sentences.

Week 4 vocabulary

Key Words

Use these words when explaining why one description is stronger than another.

descriptivereporting facts or observable information.
preciseexact and specific rather than vague.
objectivebased on evidence rather than personal reaction.
quantifyexpress something using a number, amount or measurement.
trendthe general direction of change in data.
proportiona part or share of a whole.
nominalisationexpressing an action or quality as a noun.
jargonspecialised vocabulary used by a particular field.
Core skill

Vague → Precise

The handout repeatedly emphasises exact technical terms, measurements and magnitudes.

1. WHAT?Name the exact component, variable, material, method or result.
2. HOW MUCH?Add values, units, percentages, ranges or dimensions.
3. WHAT CHANGED?Use an accurate verb: increased, decreased, stabilised, peaked, failed.
4. UNDER WHAT CONDITION?State time, load, temperature, frequency, sample or comparison point.
Vague sentenceImproved descriptive sentenceWhy it is stronger
The results were good.The results showed a 25% increase in tensile strength compared with the untreated control sample.Variable + exact percentage + comparison.
The system is small.The prototype occupies 10 cm² on the printed circuit board, reducing the footprint by 65% compared with the previous design.Exact size + location + quantified comparison.
The data changed quickly.The output voltage increased sharply from 3.0 V to 5.0 V within the first 10 seconds and then stabilised at 4.8 V.Variable + direction + values + time + final state.
Many students responded.83% (100 of 120) of participants returned the questionnaire.Exact proportion replaces “many”.
We looked at the problem.The problem was analysed using finite-element modelling to compute the strain distribution under cyclic loading.Specific method + purpose.
The graph is interesting.The graph shows a steady decline in battery capacity after 500 cycles, from 95% to 60%.Observable feature replaces personal opinion.
Formal and objective tone

Report Evidence, Not Reaction

The source handout recommends formal, impersonal language and avoiding colloquial or emotive wording.

Less Academic

The converter got really hot.

The new design worked amazingly well.

A lot of energy was lost.

More Academic

The converter temperature increased to 86°C under full-load operation.

The redesigned controller reduced settling time from 180 ms to 115 ms.

Energy loss increased by 12% at the maximum load condition.

Source rule vs course note: the handout recommends avoiding first-person pronouns and using passive constructions for an impersonal style. In published engineering writing, journal conventions vary. For this course, the priority is objective, precise, evidence-based wording; use first person or passive voice according to the convention required by your supervisor or target journal.
Language tools

Word Choice, Tense, Nominalisation and Clear Sentences

These points come directly from the source lecture, but they support the main goal: accurate description.

Precise Nouns

  • voltage, current, resistance, efficiency
  • prototype, converter, controller, sensor
  • deviation, fluctuation, failure, reduction
  • load, sample, measurement, response

Accurate Verbs

  • increase, decrease, stabilise, peak
  • measure, record, analyse, compute
  • occupy, reduce, indicate, reveal
  • fail, improve, vary, remain

Useful Modifiers

  • slight, steady, gradual, sharp
  • approximately, nearly, just over
  • maximum, minimum, average
  • measured, calculated, nominal

Tense

Present: established knowledge and current figures/tables.

Figure 3 shows the measured output voltage.

Past: completed experiments, methods and observations.

The samples were heated to 120°C for 30 minutes.

Nominalisation

Verb-based: The device failed to operate because the voltage dropped.

Nominalised: Device failure resulted from a voltage drop.

Use selectively. The handout presents nominalisation as a formal-style tool; clarity remains more important than turning every verb into a noun.

Sentence structure from the handout: avoid strings of very short sentences and excessively long sentences. Use connectors such as however, furthermore and as a result when they make the relationship between ideas explicit. Detailed sentence building comes later in Week 7 and cohesion in Week 9.

Core class practice

Build Stronger Technical Descriptions

Try each activity first. Then use Fill solution to compare.

Core today: vague → precise → quantified → objective → correctly tensed. Extra drills are kept at the end so the main lesson stays focused.

Activity A — Rewrite Vague Sentences

  1. The temperature went up.
  2. The materials performed better.
  3. The graph is interesting.

Activity B — Precision Builder

Weak sentence: The inverter worked better after the controller was changed.

Activity C — Choose the Tense

  1. Figure 2 ______ the efficiency curve. (show)
  2. During the test, the current ______ from 2.1 A to 3.4 A. (increase)
  3. The samples ______ at 120°C for 30 minutes. (heat)
  4. The results ______ a 12% reduction in power loss. (indicate)

Activity D — Describe Quantitative Data

Energy consumption: A = 120 kWh · B = 100 kWh · C = 145 kWh.

Write two accurate comparison sentences.

Formative homework

150-Word Method or Result Description

This is the source lecture's homework task, expanded with a clearer checking procedure.

Write approximately 150 words describing a method or result from your current research, a recent laboratory experiment, or a technical study you know well.

  1. Use precise nouns and accurate verbs.
  2. Quantify changes or differences using values and units where available.
  3. Use at least three suitable phrases for trends or quantities only when the data support them.
  4. Maintain a formal, objective tone.
  5. Use present tense for established knowledge/current figures and past tense for completed methods/results.
