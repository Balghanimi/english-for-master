Week 4 memory rule

Describe What the Reader Can Observe or Measure

A strong description answers more than “Was it good?”

WHAT?Name the exact component, variable, material, method or result.
HOW MUCH?Give values, units, percentages, ranges or magnitudes.
HOW DID IT CHANGE?Use an accurate verb or modifier: increased, declined, stabilised, peaked.
UNDER WHAT CONDITION?State time, load, temperature, frequency, sample or comparison point.
Weak: The results were good. Stronger: The treated sample showed a 25% increase in tensile strength compared with the untreated control.
Formal and objective tone

Remove Conversation, Emotion and Unnecessary Personal Focus

The source handout recommends formal, impersonal description and precise reporting of facts and figures.

Less Academic

We looked at the problem and got really good results.

The device was super small and worked amazingly well.

A lot of current went through the circuit.

More Academic

The problem was analysed using finite-element modelling.

The prototype occupied 10 cm² of PCB area, reducing the footprint by 65% relative to the previous design.

The measured current increased from 2.1 A to 3.4 A under full-load operation.

Course note: impersonal language is useful, but first person is not automatically wrong in every journal or discipline. Follow the style expected by your department, supervisor or target journal. The main goal here is objective, evidence-based description.
Precision and clarity

Vague → Descriptive

Use the most accurate technical term; do not choose rare synonyms merely to sound academic.

Vague sentenceImproved descriptive sentenceWhat improved?
The results were good.The results showed a 25% increase in tensile strength compared with the untreated control sample.Exact variable + percentage + comparison.
The system is small.The prototype occupies 10 cm² on the printed circuit board, reducing the footprint by 65% compared with the previous design.Exact size + location + quantified comparison.
The data changed quickly.The output voltage increased sharply from 3.0 V to 5.0 V within the first 10 seconds and then stabilised at 4.8 V.Variable + direction + values + time + final state.
Many students responded.83% (100 of 120) of participants returned the questionnaire.Exact proportion replaces “many”.
We looked at the problem.The problem was analysed using finite-element modelling to compute the strain distribution under cyclic loading.Specific method + purpose.
Word choice

Precise Nouns, Verbs and Modifiers

Choose words that tell the reader exactly what happened.

Nouns

  • voltage, current, resistance, efficiency
  • prototype, converter, controller, sensor
  • deviation, fluctuation, failure, reduction
  • load, sample, measurement, response

Verbs

  • increase, decrease, stabilise, peak
  • measure, record, analyse, compute
  • occupy, reduce, indicate, reveal
  • fail, improve, vary, remain

Modifiers

  • slight, steady, gradual, sharp
  • approximately, nearly, just over
  • maximum, minimum, average
  • measured, calculated, nominal
Define jargon when it first appears. Technical vocabulary is appropriate when it is necessary and accurate; unnecessary jargon makes a description harder to read.
Formal style tool

Nominalisation — Use It Selectively

Nominalisation turns an action into a noun and can place emphasis on the concept.

Verb-based

The device failed to operate because the voltage dropped.

Nominalised

Device failure resulted from a voltage drop.

Do not nominalise everything. Use the form that makes the technical relationship clearest.
Week 3 connection

Tense in Descriptive Writing

Week 4 applies the tense choices you practised in Week 3.

Present Simple

Use for established knowledge and for figures/tables in the current document.

Figure 3 shows the measured output voltage.
Copper conducts electricity efficiently.

Past Simple

Use for a specific experiment, completed method or completed observation.

The samples were heated to 120°C for 30 minutes.
The output voltage increased by 18% during the test.

Core class practice

Improve the Description

Do the task first. Then use Fill solution to compare.

Core today: precision, objectivity, quantification and tense. Deeper graph-language drills are saved for Weeks 5–6.

Activity A — Rewrite Vague Sentences

  1. The temperature went up.
  2. The materials performed better.
  3. The graph is interesting.

Activity B — Formal and Objective

  1. We looked at the problem using a computer model.
  2. The converter got really hot during the test.
  3. A lot of energy was lost.

Activity C — Choose the Tense

  1. Figure 2 ______ the efficiency curve. (show)
  2. During the test, the current ______ from 2.1 A to 3.4 A. (increase)
  3. The samples ______ at 120°C for 30 minutes. (heat)
  4. The results ______ a 12% reduction in power loss. (indicate)

Activity D — Describe Quantitative Data

Energy consumption: System A = 120 kWh · System B = 100 kWh · System C = 145 kWh.

Write two sentences describing the differences.

Formative homework

150-Word Method or Result Description

This follows the homework task in the source lecture.

Write approximately 150 words describing a method or result from your current research, a recent laboratory experiment, or a technical study you are familiar with.

  1. Use precise nouns and accurate verbs.
  2. Quantify changes or differences using values and units where available.
  3. Use at least three suitable descriptive phrases for trends or quantities.
  4. Maintain a formal, objective tone.
  5. Choose tense deliberately: present for established knowledge/current figures; past for completed methods/results.
