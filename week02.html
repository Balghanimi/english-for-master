Week 2 reference

Key Words for Vocabulary in Context

These terms are the language you need to talk about how you inferred a meaning.

contextthe words and ideas surrounding a word
context clueevidence that helps reveal a meaning
inferreach a likely meaning from evidence
inferencethe meaning or conclusion you infer
definitiona direct explanation of meaning
exemplificationexamples that clarify a category or idea
contrastan opposite or different idea used as a clue
signal worda word such as however or such as that points to a clue
provisionaltemporary; acceptable until more evidence is available
word familyrelated forms such as analyse, analysis, analytical
evidencethe exact phrase, example, number, or contrast supporting your guess
verifycheck your inferred meaning after you have made a guess
Useful answer frame: “I infer that _____ means _____ because _____ provides a definition / example / contrast / general-knowledge clue.”

Reading strategy

A Reader's Strategy for an Unfamiliar Word

The handout's central advice is simple: do not stop immediately.

When You Meet a New Word

Keep reading to the end of the sentence.
Read the next sentence as well.
If the general meaning is still unclear, return to the unfamiliar word.
Look for a definition, example, contrast, or useful topic knowledge.
Make a provisional guess that fits the context.

Context clue types

Four Types of Context Clue

These are the four clue types used in the original lecture document.

1. Definition

The writer explains the word directly, often after a dash, or, commas, is, or means.

The media — organisations such as newspapers and television that provide news and information — influence how people receive current events.
Clue: the meaning is given immediately after the word.

2. Exemplification

The writer gives examples that show what the word includes.

Symbols can communicate ideas. For example, a heart can represent love.
Clue: for example introduces an example.

3. Contrast

An opposite or different idea helps reveal the meaning.

The teacher considered eliminating one question; however, he finally decided to keep it.
Clue: however contrasts eliminating with keep.

4. General Knowledge

Knowledge of the topic or situation helps you interpret the word.

News reports described the devastation after a major earthquake and tsunami.
Clue: knowledge of the disaster suggests serious destruction.
General / real-world examples

Context Clues Beyond Engineering

The original lecture uses ordinary academic and real-life situations. These examples help you practise the skill without depending on specialist knowledge.

Education — contrast:
Mina felt calm before the exam; however, when she saw the first page, she became agitated.
Key words: calm · however · agitated
Journalism — examples:
A journalist must be versatile. For instance, the job may require writing, interviewing, speaking, researching, and working quickly.
Key words: for instance · many different skills
Work — contrast:
His office job was sedentary; in contrast, his new reporting job kept him away from his desk most of the day.
Key words: in contrast · away from his desk
Everyday technology — example:
Many people prefer portable devices, such as phones, tablets, and small music players that can be carried easily.
Key words: such as · carried easily

Useful clue: endings such as -tion / -sion / -ity often mark nouns, -al / -ive often mark adjectives, and -ly often marks adverbs. These are tendencies, not perfect rules.

The voltage deviation, or difference between the measured and reference voltage, remained below 1.5%. Unlike the earlier controller, which became unstable when the load changed rapidly, the revised controller was robust and maintained acceptable performance across all three tested loads. The steady-state current was approximately 5 A, with measured values ranging from 4.8 A to 5.2 A.
