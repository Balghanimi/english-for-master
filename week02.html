A Reader's Strategy for an Unfamiliar Word

The handout's central advice is simple: do not stop immediately.

When You Meet a New Word

Keep reading to the end of the sentence.
Read the next sentence as well.
If the general meaning is still unclear, return to the unfamiliar word.
Look for a definition, example, contrast, or useful topic knowledge.
Make a provisional guess that fits the context.

Warm-Up — Do Not Use a Dictionary

Guess the underlined word. Write the evidence that helped you.

1. The output was attenuated; its amplitude fell from 5.0 V to 1.4 V after the filter was connected.
2. The fault was intermittent. It appeared during three start-ups but was absent during the remaining tests.
3. The reading was negligible, only 0.02 A compared with the 8 A rated current.
4. The enclosure is transparent, so the internal wiring can be seen without opening it.

Four Types of Context Clue

The same four clue types from the handout are applied here to engineering language.

1. Definition

The writer tells you what the unfamiliar word means.

A transient, or a short-duration change in voltage or current, may occur during switching.
Clue: the definition follows or.

2. Exemplification

Examples show what belongs to the meaning.

Nonlinear loads, such as rectifiers and variable-speed drives, can introduce harmonics.
Clue: examples follow such as.

3. Contrast

An opposite or different idea helps reveal the meaning.

Unlike a stable output, an oscillatory response repeatedly rises and falls.
Clue: unlike sets up the contrast.

4. General Knowledge

Your knowledge of the topic helps you interpret the word.

The heat sink reached 105 °C, and the technician stopped the test to prevent damage because the device had overheated.
Clue: the technical situation supports the meaning.

Signal Hunt

Underline the signal word or phrase. Then write the clue type.

  1. A sag, or a short reduction in RMS voltage, may last from half a cycle to one minute.
  2. Several protective devices, such as fuses and circuit breakers, interrupt excessive current.
  3. The open-loop response was slow; however, the closed-loop response reached the reference in 0.18 s.
  4. Unlike a conductor, an insulator strongly limits electric current.

Core Class Practice

These are the Week 2 tasks to complete in class.

Core today: Activities A and B. The later Extra Practice section is optional / fast-finisher work.

Activity A — Identify the Clue Type

Choose definition, exemplification, contrast, or general knowledge. Highlight the clue in the sentence.

  1. A thermistor is a resistor whose resistance changes strongly with temperature.
  2. Some renewable sources, for instance solar and wind power, are naturally variable.
  3. The measured current was steady at first. However, after the fault, it became erratic and changed unpredictably.
  4. The cable insulation was brittle; it cracked when it was bent during inspection.
  5. A redundant sensor, that is, a second sensor installed as backup, can improve reliability.
  6. Whereas the first waveform was smooth, the second was distorted and contained several sharp peaks.
Answer key: reviewed in class.

Activity B — Infer the Meaning

Choose the most likely meaning from the context. Then write the evidence that supports your choice.

1. The controller response was sluggish; it required 4.8 s to reach the reference, whereas the improved controller required 0.7 s.
2. The enclosure is impermeable to water, meaning that water cannot pass through it.
3. The signal was periodic. For example, the same waveform repeated every 20 ms.
4. The first battery showed severe degradation after 300 cycles. In contrast, the second retained 94% of its original capacity.
5. The engineer made an estimate of the cable length because an exact measurement was not available.
Answers are checked in class; the public page does not contain the answer key.

Useful clue: endings such as -tion / -sion / -ity often mark nouns, -al / -ive often mark adjectives, and -ly often marks adverbs. These are tendencies, not perfect rules.

Activity C — Choose the Correct Family Member

  1. The controller improved voltage ________. (regulate)
  2. The output remained ________ after 0.3 s. (stability)
  3. The measured values ________ between 219 and 222 V. (variation)
  4. Harmonic ________ was performed using FFT. (analyse)
  5. The current was ________ 4.8 A. (approximation)
  6. Load current is a ________ that changes with operating condition. (vary)
Answer key: reviewed in class.

Applied Mini-Reading: Infer Before You Look Up

Use the whole paragraph, not just the sentence containing the unfamiliar word.

During the inverter test, an intermittent switching fault appeared during only four of twenty start-up events and was absent during the remaining trials. The team added an LC filter to mitigate the output ripple; after installation, ripple decreased from 6.1% to 2.0%. The voltage deviation, or difference between the measured and reference voltage, remained below 1.5%. Unlike the earlier controller, which became unstable when the load changed rapidly, the revised controller was robust and maintained acceptable performance across all three tested loads. The steady-state current was approximately 5 A, with measured values ranging from 4.8 A to 5.2 A.

Extra A — Context-Clue Challenge

  2. The output was initially erratic. However, after tuning, it became consistent and predictable.
