Week 3 language

Key Words

Use these terms when explaining a tense choice.

general statement

A statement treated as true at the time of writing.

completed event

An action or study located at a finished time in the past.

current relevance

A past action or development connected to the present.

prediction

A statement about what is expected in the future.

specific past time

A finished time marker such as in 2023 or last year.

time period up to now

A continuing period such as since 2022 or over the last decade.

active voice

The subject performs the action.

passive voice

The subject receives the action or the action/result is foregrounded.

Language review

The Four Core Academic Tenses

Academic tense choice depends on what the writer is doing.

Present Simple

current truthinterpretationaimfigures
  • general statements true at the time of writing
  • interpretations of findings
  • aims of reports or sections
  • references to tables, figures, charts, and graphs
EE: Figure 2 shows the inverter output voltage.
AI: The results suggest that the model performs better with additional training data.

Past Simple

specific pastcompleted studypast method/result
  • findings reported at a specific finished time
  • completed methods, data collection, and results
  • older ideas or findings discussed as past positions
EE: In 2024, the team tested three converter prototypes.
Space: The satellite entered orbit after launch.

Present Perfect

past → presentresearch summarydevelopment
  • connect past developments with the present
  • summarise previous research
  • emphasise what has been achieved
  • introduce a topic, limitation, or conclusion with present relevance
EE: Researchers have improved battery management algorithms over the last decade.
AI: Several studies have examined AI-based fault detection.

Future with will

predictionfuture purpose
  • predict future findings, developments, or results
  • state what a report or section will do
EE: Future grids will require more flexible energy storage.
Report: This section will compare the two control strategies.
Important: the same topic can move across tenses because the writer's purpose changes. Compare: “The system operates at 50 Hz” (general/current) vs “The system operated at 50 Hz during the test” (completed experiment).
Fast decision tool

Which Tense Should I Consider First?

True/current/figure?Start with present simple.
Finished past time?Start with past simple.
Past connected to now?Start with present perfect.
Prediction/future purpose?Start with will + base verb.
Active and passive

All Four Tenses Can Be Active or Passive

Voice changes the focus; it does not change the tense.

Active

Present: The sensor measures temperature.

Past: The researchers measured temperature.

Present perfect: Engineers have reduced switching losses.

Future: The controller will regulate the output.

Passive

Present: Temperature is measured by the sensor.

Past: Temperature was measured during the test.

Present perfect: Switching losses have been reduced.

Future: The output will be regulated by the controller.

Academic writing does not require passive voice all the time. Use it when the method, process, data, or result is more important than the actor.

Quick Practice — Change to Passive

  1. The oscilloscope displays the waveform.
  2. The research team measured the current at 5 kHz.
  3. Engineers have reduced the converter losses.
  4. The new controller will regulate the DC-link voltage.
Guided practice

Choose Tense from Academic Purpose

These activities follow the progression of the source worksheets.

Activity A — Present Simple or Past Simple?

Write the correct form of the verb.

  1. The IEEE 802.11 family ______ wireless local-area networking standards. (define)
  2. In 2024, the laboratory team ______ three inverter prototypes. (test)
  3. Figure 3 ______ the measured output current. (show)
  4. Early simulation models ______ ideal switching behaviour. (assume)
  5. The present findings ______ that efficiency improves above 80% load. (suggest)

Activity B — Present Simple or Present Perfect?

  1. Numerous studies ______ AI-based fault diagnosis in power systems. (investigate)
  2. This report ______ the effect of load variation on converter efficiency. (examine)
  3. Researchers ______ several low-cost methods for estimating battery state of charge. (develop)
  4. Figure 4 ______ the temperature profile during steady-state operation. (show)
  5. Over the last decade, photovoltaic module costs ______ substantially. (fall)

Activity C — Past Simple or Present Perfect?

  1. The team ______ the first prototype in March 2024. (install)
  2. Since March 2024, the prototype ______ continuously in the laboratory. (operate)
  3. Last year, the researchers ______ a peak efficiency of 96.1%. (record)
  4. Over the last five years, several studies ______ similar stability problems. (report)
  5. During yesterday's test, the sensor ______ a maximum temperature of 78°C. (measure)

Activity D — Present Simple or Future with will?

Some sentences can accept both forms; the rhetorical meaning changes.

  1. This report ______ the performance of two MPPT methods. (compare)
  2. In the next decade, electricity demand ______ in many rapidly growing cities. (increase)
  3. The results ______ that further testing is necessary. (suggest)
  4. This section ______ the limitations of the proposed method. (discuss)
  5. Future satellite networks ______ more autonomous routing decisions. (require)
Integrated grammar

Tense Review

Use only present simple, past simple, present perfect simple, or future simple — active or passive.

  1. Electrical power ______ to the laboratory through a protected distribution panel. (supply)
  2. In 2022, the research group ______ a new fault-detection algorithm. (develop)
  3. Over the last three years, engineers ______ the algorithm's accuracy. (improve)
  4. Next year, the laboratory ______ a higher-power test platform. (install)
  5. Figure 5 ______ the relationship between load current and efficiency. (show)
  6. During the experiment, output voltage ______ every 10 ms. (record)
  7. Since the revised controller was introduced, settling time ______ by 35%. (decrease)
  8. It is predicted that energy-storage costs ______ further. (fall)
  9. This study ______ three major limitations of the proposed design. (identify)
  10. Generally, induction motors ______ efficiently near their rated operating point. (operate)

Error clinic

Find Five Tense Errors

Correct the tense only when the academic time/function requires it.

Over the last decade, satellite-based communication systems became more accessible to universities and small research teams. In 2024, one student team has tested a low-cost ground-station receiver. The experiment records signal strength over fourteen days. Figure 2 showed the average daily signal-to-noise ratio. The team expects that improved software filtering reduced interference in future versions.

Freer practice

Write with All Four Tenses

This adapts the freer-practice task from the source worksheet to your subject area.

Choose One Topic

AI for electrical fault detection · satellite communication · renewable-energy integration · battery management · electric vehicles · your MSc topic.

Write 100–130 words and use all four core tenses at least once. Underline the verb phrase for each tense.

Before You Finish

  • Present simple: a current/general statement or figure.
  • Past simple: a completed study, test, method, or result.
  • Present perfect: research/development connected to now.
  • Future with will: a prediction or future report purpose.
Do not force a tense. Each form should have a clear academic reason.
