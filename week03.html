Week 3 language

Key Words

Use these terms when explaining a tense choice.

general statement

A statement treated as true at the time of writing.

completed event

An action or study located at a finished time in the past.

current relevance

A past action or development connected to the present.

prediction

A statement about what is expected in the future.

specific past time

A finished time marker such as in 2023 or last year.

time period up to now

A continuing period such as since 2022 or over the last decade.

active voice

The subject performs the action.

passive voice

The subject receives the action or the action/result is foregrounded.

Language review

The Four Core Academic Tenses

Academic tense choice depends on what the writer is doing.

Present Simple

current truthinterpretationaimfigures
  • general statements true at the time of writing
  • interpretations of findings
  • aims of reports or sections
  • references to tables, figures, charts, and graphs
EE: Figure 2 shows the inverter output voltage.
AI: The results suggest that the model performs better with additional training data.

Past Simple

specific pastcompleted studypast method/result
  • findings reported at a specific finished time
  • completed methods, data collection, and results
  • older ideas or findings discussed as past positions
EE: In 2024, the team tested three converter prototypes.
Space: The satellite entered orbit after launch.

Present Perfect

past → presentresearch summarydevelopment
  • connect past developments with the present
  • summarise previous research
  • emphasise what has been achieved
  • introduce a topic, limitation, or conclusion with present relevance
EE: Researchers have improved battery management algorithms over the last decade.
AI: Several studies have examined AI-based fault detection.

Future with will

predictionfuture purpose
  • predict future findings, developments, or results
  • state what a report or section will do
EE: Future grids will require more flexible energy storage.
Report: This section will compare the two control strategies.
Important: the same topic can move across tenses because the writer's purpose changes. Compare: “The system operates at 50 Hz” (general/current) vs “The system operated at 50 Hz during the test” (completed experiment).
The Week 3 memory rule

Choose Tense from the Sentence's Job

Section names help, but the function of the sentence decides the tense.

FACT / CURRENT CLAIM → PRESENTWhat is true now; what the paper, figure, or result says now.
RESEARCH UP TO NOW → PRESENT PERFECTWhat researchers have studied, developed, or established with present relevance.
COMPLETED STUDY → PASTWhat researchers did, measured, tested, or found in a finished study.
FUTURE / POSSIBILITY → WILL, MAY, COULDA prediction, planned next step, or cautious future possibility.
Most important rule: a research paragraph can change tense when the academic purpose changes. Do not keep one tense just because the paragraph is inside one section.

Authentic research-paper language

How Tense Works Across a Real Paper

This is a typical tendency map for engineering papers, not a rigid rule.

Real papers used:
1) Hallmann, Pietracho & Komarnicki (2024), Comparison of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning Methods Used in Electric Power System Operation, Energies 17(11), 2790. Open paper ↗

2) Yoon & Yoon (2024), Development of a real-time fault detection method for electric power system via transformer-based deep learning model, International Journal of Electrical Power & Energy Systems 159, 110069. Open paper ↗
Academic jobTypical tense choiceWhy?What the real papers illustrate
Introduction: fact, definition, current problem, paper aimPresent simple · frequentThe writer presents information as current or valid at the time of writing.Hallmann et al. use present forms for the current purpose and scope of the paper.
Introduction: research trend continuing to nowPresent perfect · frequent when relevantEarlier work is connected to the present research situation.Hallmann et al. use present-perfect language to connect long-running AI research with the current field.
Introduction: specific earlier study/eventPast simpleThe writer points to a finished, dated event or a particular earlier study.The Hallmann Introduction also shifts to past simple for a specific historical event.
Method / procedurePast simple · often passiveThe procedure is usually completed before the paper is written.Yoon & Yoon's abstract reports completed data preparation using past passive forms. This is authentic method-style language, even though the quoted wording appears in the abstract.
Result of a completed experimentPast simpleThe writer reports what happened during testing.Yoon & Yoon's abstract reports a completed classification outcome in past simple.
Figure / table / current interpretationPresent simpleThe figure exists in the current paper, and the writer explains what the result means now.Use patterns such as Figure 2 shows… and The results indicate… when referring to the current text or present interpretation.
DiscussionPresent + past mixThe writer moves between what happened and what it means now.A tense change inside one discussion paragraph can therefore be correct and purposeful.
ConclusionPresent / present perfect + past recapThe writer states current significance, summarises what has been established, and may recap completed work.A conclusion is not automatically future tense. Future forms are mainly for future work, prediction, or recommendation.
Do not memorise “Introduction = present” or “Conclusion = future.” Instead ask: Is this sentence a current claim, research up to now, a completed study, or a future possibility?

Paragraph-level control

One Paragraph Can Mix Tenses Correctly

Artificial intelligence plays an increasing role in power-system operation. Researchers have investigated machine-learning methods for several decades. In one completed experiment, the researchers trained a transformer-based model and tested it on simulated signals. The findings suggest that this approach can support faster monitoring. Further validation may be needed under real operating conditions.
Why the tense changes: present = current claim/interpretation; present perfect = research up to now; past = completed study; modal = cautious future implication.
Short support point

Active and Passive: Keep the Tense, Change the Focus

Voice matters, but it is not the main target of Week 3.

Completed method — past

Active: The researchers measured the current.

Passive: The current was measured during the test.

Research up to now — present perfect

Active: Engineers have developed several methods.

Passive: Several methods have been developed.

Use passive when the procedure, data, or result is more important than the actor. Do not use passive simply because the writing is academic.

Two-Sentence Check

  1. The research team measured the current at 5 kHz.
  2. Engineers have developed several fault-detection methods.

Application

Write by Section Purpose — Do Not Force Every Tense

Use only the tenses that the sentence purpose actually needs.

A. Introduction Language

Write 70–90 words about one engineering/AI topic. Include:

  • one current fact or problem → usually present simple
  • one research trend up to now → usually present perfect
  • a specific earlier study/event only if it is genuinely relevant → past simple

B. Method + Result Language

Write 4–5 sentences about a completed experiment or test. Use past simple for the completed procedure/result. Use present simple only if you refer to a current figure/table or interpret what the result means now.

Future forms are optional. Add will / may / could only if you genuinely make a prediction, recommendation, or future-work statement.

Formative homework

Two Short Academic Sections

The goal is appropriate tense choice, not using every tense once.

Part A — Introduction · 90–110 words

Choose an Electrical Engineering, AI, space-technology, or MSc topic. Write a short Introduction paragraph containing:

  • a current fact/problem in present simple;
  • a research trend or development connected to now in present perfect;
  • past simple only if you mention a specific finished study or dated event.

Part B — Method/Result · 80–100 words

Describe a completed test, simulation, experiment, or published study. Use past simple for completed actions/results. You may use present simple for a figure/table reference or present interpretation.

  1. Underline the main verb phrase in each sentence.
  2. Write a short note above each underline: current fact / research up to now / completed study / current interpretation / future possibility.
  3. If you add future work, choose will, may, or could according to certainty.
  4. Swap with a partner and check whether every tense has a clear reason.
Bring to Week 4: both short sections and the peer check. This is formative practice, not the graded 5-mark homework.
