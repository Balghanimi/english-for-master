Key focus

Five Ideas to Remember

These are the messages students should remember after the first lecture.

1. FormalAcademic English should not sound casual or conversational.
2. ObjectiveUse evidence, observations and measured results.
3. PrecisePrefer numbers, units and exact terms over vague impressions.
4. ClearGood academic English is easy to follow, not unnecessarily complicated.
5. TechnicalUse the correct disciplinary term, not a rare synonym chosen only to sound advanced.
Key message for students:

Academic English does not mean difficult English. It means language that is appropriate, accurate, precise, clear and evidence-based.

18–33 minutes

What Makes English Academic?

Academic language is selected for a specific audience, purpose and discipline.

Formal

Avoid casual or conversational expressions such as stuff, a lot of, and really big when more precise alternatives are available.

Objective

Report evidence, measurements and observations without unnecessary exaggeration or emotional judgement.

Precise

Use numbers, quantities, units, technical terms and specific verbs rather than general impressions.

Clear

Use controlled sentence structure so the reader can understand the intended meaning efficiently.

Academic ≠ Complicated

Do not choose a long or unusual word merely to sound academic. Choose the word that expresses the meaning most accurately.

Better principle:clear + precise + appropriate is stronger than “complex for the sake of complexity.”

Objectivity ≠ No First Person Ever

Different disciplines have different conventions. The practical rule for this course is to avoid unnecessary personal or emotional wording and to keep the focus on evidence and technical meaning.

Roadmap: Week 2 develops academic vocabulary, Week 3 tense control, Week 4 descriptive engineering language, Week 7 sentence structure, and Week 9 cohesion and coherence.

Formal Register: From Conversation to Academic English

Students identify exactly what changed between the informal and academic versions.

Less academic / vagueMore academic / controlledWhat changed?
We looked at the problem.The fault was investigated using time-domain simulation in MATLAB/Simulink.Vague verb → specific method.
The passive voice is a choice here, not a rule.
The results were good.The proposed controller reduced total harmonic distortion from 8.4% to 2.1%.Evaluation → measured evidence.
The system is small.The prototype occupies 10 cm² of PCB area, 65% less than the previous design.Adjective → dimension + comparison.
The signal changed quickly.The output voltage rose from 3.0 V to 5.0 V within 10 s, then settled at 4.8 V (±2%).Impression → values + trend + tolerance.
The motor got hot.The stator winding temperature reached 78 °C after 30 min at rated load.Informal verb → quantity + condition.
Key pointAcademic ≠ passive. The first example uses the passive because the method is more important than the researcher. First person can still be appropriate when disciplinary conventions allow it.

Precision, Clarity & Cautious Claims

Strong technical writing uses observable, measurable information and avoids claims that are stronger than the evidence.

Use Exact Information

  • 3.3 V rather than low voltage.
  • 12 ms rather than very fast.
  • 83% rather than many participants.
  • 10 cm² rather than small.
Key point: include a number + unit + comparison or condition whenever it genuinely improves meaning.

Use Accurate Technical Vocabulary

Choose the most accurate term rather than a rare synonym merely to sound sophisticated.

Prefer:voltage fluctuation when that is the technical phenomenon.
Avoid: replacing a correct engineering term with an approximate “advanced” synonym.
Define: unfamiliar abbreviations and specialist terms when first introduced.

Cautious Academic Claims — Hedging

Objectivity also means matching the strength of your language to the strength of the evidence.

The results show that X causes Y.
The results suggest that X contributes to Y.
mayappears tois likely totends toapproximatelythese findings indicate
QuestionWhat evidence would justify “causes”? Then contrast it with suggests or is associated with.

Academic English in a Real Electrical Engineering Paper

This example is used for language analysis only: students observe how published engineers express purpose, caution, technical precision and evidence.

Open-access paper

Xiu-Hua Guo, Che-Wei Chang & Le-Ren Chang-Chien (2022)
Digital Implementation of Harmonic and Unbalanced Load Compensation for Voltage Source Inverter to Operate in Grid Forming Microgrid, Electronics, 11(6), 886.

Electrical Engineering context: voltage-source inverter (VSI), grid-forming microgrid, FFT, proportional-resonant control, total harmonic distortion (THD), predictive current control, and experimental verification.

Short phrase from the paperLanguage featureWhat to notice
“is presented in this paper”Formal purpose / reporting languageThe writer states what the paper introduces without conversational wording.
“could be reduced”Hedging + passive voiceCould makes the claim more cautious; the passive keeps attention on the technical effect.
“to mitigate the total harmonic distortion”Purpose + precise technical vocabularyThe purpose is expressed with to + verb, and the technical quantity is named precisely.
“is verified through experiments”Evidence languageThe claim is connected to experimental evidence, not personal opinion.

Language watchlist

Common Issues to Watch For

Especially useful for Arabic-speaking EFL writers. These are common transfer patterns, not problems every student will have.

1. Articles

Use the when the noun is specific or already identified.

The voltage across R1 was measured.

2. Uncountable Nouns

research, equipment, software, information normally do not take a plural -s in these meanings.

3. Verb Patterns

discuss about → discuss
explain me → explain to me

4. Comma Splices

The voltage dropped, the device failed.

The voltage dropped; the device failed.
or The device failed because the voltage dropped.

5. Tense Choice

Past for completed procedures/results: was measured.
Present for facts and what a figure shows: Figure 2 shows…

6. Noun Order

English technical noun groups place modifiers before the main noun:

a three-phase induction motor drive

Quick Academic Word Choices

Choose the word that is most accurate for the meaning; do not replace words mechanically.

Often too general / informalPossible academic / precise alternative
getobtain, receive, produce — depending on meaning
a lot ofnumerous (countable) or, better, an exact value such as 43%
thingcomponent, parameter, factor, device, variable
goodeffective, accurate, stable, within specification — if evidence supports it
baddegraded, inadequate, unstable, outside specification
look atexamine, analyse, evaluate, investigate
bighigh, large, substantial + an exact value where possible
nowadayscurrently, in recent years — when the time reference is accurate
So / But / And at sentence startWhen a more formal connector is useful: Therefore / However / In addition. Starting with But or And is not automatically wrong.
