Cohesion & Coherence: From Sentences to Research Paragraphs

Syllabus Week 9: Cohesion and Coherence. You will practise four skills (reference, transitions, lexical cohesion and logical flow) by revising Results/Discussion paragraphs like the ones in your thesis.

MSc Electrical Engineering60-minute teaching editionResearch-writing workshopFormative only
The progression from Week 7

Week 7 sentence control → Week 9 paragraph cohesion and coherence

Week 7: write one accurate sentence. Week 9: link sentences so that a whole paragraph makes one clear point that your evidence supports.

Carry forward your Week 7 writing

Bring your two revised sentences from Week 7 and your Week 6 Results/Discussion paragraph. Today you will join them into one clear paragraph and explain your changes. (Week 8 was the midterm; this is the next teaching week.)

By the end of this session, students can

LO1 · ReferenceMake every it / this / they point to one clear thing, or use a precise noun phrase (this 0.7-percentage-point difference).
LO2 · TransitionsChoose the linking word that matches the real relationship in the data (contrast, addition or result) and punctuate it correctly.
LO3 · Lexical cohesionUse the same technical term for the same thing; never switch to a word that names a different device or variable.
LO4 · Logical flowOrder sentences from known to new information (point → evidence → interpretation → limitation) in one paragraph of your own.

Warm-up · 0–4 minutes

0 · Two drafts, one problem

In Week 7 you made single sentences accurate. Today you look at the whole paragraph. Both drafts below use linking words, but only one is safe to put in a thesis.

Two words for today

Cohesion = the visible links between sentences: this comparison, however, or the same technical term used again. Coherence = the reader can follow one main point, because every sentence is relevant and comes in a logical order. Easy test: cohesion is about the links; coherence is about the message. Whether a claim is too strong for the evidence is a third question (Week 10).

Draft A · well linked, but claims too much

At 80% load, Design B reached 96.7% efficiency and Design A reached 96.0%. Therefore, Design B's topology caused lower power losses. Furthermore, the results prove that Design B is superior in all conditions.

Draft B · well linked and careful

At 80% load, Design B reached 96.7% efficiency, exceeding Design A by 0.7 percentage points. This margin reversed the order seen at 10% load, where Design A led by 4.5 percentage points. However, only one prototype of each design was tested, and no loss breakdown was reported.

Activity A · Warm-up (4 minutes)

A1 · What can the numbers not prove?

Draft A makes two claims that the measurements cannot prove. Underline them. For each one, write the evidence that is missing.

A2 · Find the links

In Draft B, circle two linking expressions (a this + noun phrase or a linking word). For each one, write what it connects.

ExtensionA3 · Results or Discussion?

Would Draft B fit the Results section, the Discussion section, or both? Give one reason.

Check the reasoning (self-study)

A1: no measured loss breakdown supports the proposed mechanism; one operating point with one prototype per design cannot prove global superiority. A2: this margin refers to the 0.7-percentage-point difference at 80% load; however introduces the limitation (one prototype, no loss data). A3: it reports a Results comparison and adds a Discussion limitation; the lecturer may divide these moves between sections.

Key words for this week

reference: pointing back with it, this, they or a repeated noun · الإحالة

referent: the exact thing a word points to · المُحال إليه

antecedent: the earlier noun that it / this / they replaces · مرجع الضمير

transition (linking word): however, therefore, in addition · أداة الربط

summary noun: this + a noun that sums up the last sentence (this difference) · اسم تلخيصي

concession: admitting a point against your claim (however, although) · الاستدراك

qualify a claim: make a claim more limited and careful · تقييد الادعاء

limitation: what your data cannot show · محددات البحث

Reference · 4–11 minutes

1 · Make every it and this clear

Reference means pointing back to something you already wrote, using it, this, they or a repeated noun. The reader must know exactly which design, value or result you mean. In engineering writing, an unclear it can change the scientific meaning, not only the style.

Unclear draft

Design A and Design B were tested at 80% load. It reached 96.7% efficiency.

Which design does it mean? A or B? The reader cannot know.

Clear version

Design A and Design B were tested at 80% load. Design B reached 96.7% efficiency.

Two things were just mentioned, so repeat the exact noun.

Five ways to refer clearly

StrategyExampleWhen to use it
Repeat exact termDesign B ... Design BProtect the experimental referent
Pronoun after one referentThe sensor was calibrated. It then...Safe only when unambiguous
This + summary noun (with a number)This 1.2 kW discrepancy...Names the thing and its size
This finding / limitationThis single-prototype limitation...Sums up the point you just made
Figure or table referenceAs Fig. 1 shows, Design B...Links the sentence to its evidence

Before The simulation predicted 4.0 kW at 14:00; the measured mean was 2.8 kW. This was high.

After The simulation predicted 4.0 kW at 14:00; the measured mean was 2.8 kW. This 1.2 kW prediction–measurement gap requires further investigation.

Activity B · Repair the references (5 minutes)

B1 · Which design?

At 80% load, Design A and Design B achieved 96.0% and 96.7% efficiency, respectively. It was higher. Rewrite the last sentence: name the design and give the difference.

B2 · Replace a bare this

At 14:00, the predicted output exceeded the measured mean by 1.2 kW. This requires investigation. Rewrite the second sentence: replace This with This + a noun that names the difference.

ExtensionB3 · Start with the limitation

One prototype of each converter design was evaluated. They cannot establish between-unit variability. Rewrite the second sentence so that it starts with the limitation: Testing only one prototype ...

Self-check models

B1: Design B was 0.7 percentage points more efficient than Design A at 80% load. B2: This 1.2 kW prediction–measurement difference requires investigation. B3: Testing only one prototype per design cannot show how much units of the same design vary.

Transitions · 11–18 minutes

2 · Link sentences without claiming too much

A transition (linking word) tells the reader how two sentences are related. In Week 7 you linked ideas inside one sentence. Now you link separate sentences. Choose the word that matches the real relationship in your data.

What you want to do → relationship → linking word

What you want to doRelationship · linking wordsSafe example
Results: compare two valuescontrast · however, whereas, by contrastDesign A led at 10% load; however, Design B led at 80% load.
Results: add a measurementaddition · in addition, alsoVoltage was logged. In addition, current was measured.
Discussion: draw a conclusionresult · therefore, soThe SD at 14:00 (0.9 kW) was three times that at 12:00 (0.3 kW); therefore, the 14:00 mean was the least precise value in Fig. 2.
Discussion: limit your claimconcession · however, althoughDesign B was more efficient at 80% load. However, only one prototype of each design was tested.
Rule: however = contrast. therefore = the second sentence follows logically from the first. A linking word cannot create a reason that your data does not show. Punctuation: “Sentence 1; however, sentence 2.” or “Sentence 1. However, sentence 2.”

Activity C · Choose the right link (5 minutes)

C1 · Fill the gap

Design A led at 10% load. ______, Design B led at 80% load. Write However or Therefore. Then name the relationship: contrast or result?

C2 · Is this “therefore” logical?

Only one prototype per design was evaluated; therefore, the sample cannot show how much units of the same design vary. Is the conclusion logical? Explain in one sentence.

C3 · Repair a false result link

“The predicted PV output was 4.0 kW; therefore, the observed mean was 2.8 kW.” Replace therefore with a better linking word. Explain why therefore is wrong here.

ExtensionC4 · Punctuate the link

The efficiency difference was 0.7 percentage points at 80% load however no repeat tests were reported. Rewrite using a semicolon and a comma.

Self-check reasoning

C1: However (By contrast or On the other hand are also correct): contrast, because the more efficient design changes with load. C2: Yes; one tested unit per design cannot show variation between units. C3: Use whereas / but / however; the prediction does not cause the measured value. C4: The efficiency difference was 0.7 percentage points at 80% load; however, no repeat tests were reported.

Lexical cohesion · 18–24 minutes

3 · Use the same term for the same thing

Lexical cohesion means linking sentences by repeating a key word or using a closely related one. In general English, writers change words to avoid repetition. In engineering, a new word can mean a new device or variable, so repeat the exact term when you mean the same thing.

Same thing:Design B→this design→Design B
Topic moves forward:its efficiency at 80% load→this 0.7-percentage-point advantage

Safe chain

Design B achieved 96.7% efficiency at 80% load. This measured efficiency was 0.7 percentage points higher than the value for Design A.

Unsafe substitution

The inverter was evaluated. The generator produced the same waveform.

An inverter and a generator are different devices. The reader now thinks two machines were tested.

Keep the meaning; do not change the thing

Starting termSafe chainUnsafe change (do not do this)
measurementthe measured mean → this observed valuesimulation as though it were measured
predictionthe simulated output → that predictionobserved output without distinction
prototypethis tested unit → the single prototypeall manufactured units
differencethe 1.2 kW gap → this discrepancya statistically significant difference (untested)

Activity D · Keep the term (4 minutes)

D1 · What does it point to?

In “The predicted output was 4.0 kW. The measured mean was 2.8 kW. This discrepancy was 1.2 kW,” what exactly is this discrepancy?

D2 · Repair the term

Rewrite “Design B was tested at 80% load. The generator reached 96.7% efficiency.” The text never mentioned a generator, so do not change the device name.

ExtensionD3 · Link without claiming too much

Join these facts in two sentences: Design B = 96.7% at 80% load; Design B = 96.3% at 100% load; only one prototype was tested. Use at least one precise reference.

Self-check models

D1: the 1.2 kW difference between the simulated 4.0 kW output and measured 2.8 kW mean. D2: Design B was tested at 80% load and reached 96.7% efficiency. The referent is Design B; do not introduce an unmentioned generator. D3: Design B reached 96.7% efficiency at 80% load and 96.3% at 100% load. These values come from a single prototype of this design. A simpler correct version is also fine.

Logical flow · 24–31 minutes

4 · Build a paragraph that flows

Given → new: start each sentence with something the reader already knows (often a this + noun phrase), then add one new piece of information. Every sentence must add something; a sentence that only repeats the last one breaks the flow.

1 · PointTopic sentence: state the main comparison.
2 · EvidenceReport quantities and conditions.
3 · InterpretationExplain only what follows from data.
4 · LimitationState what remains untested.

Worked example: each sentence adds something new

Point + Evidence At 80% load, Design B achieved 96.7% efficiency, compared with 96.0% for Design A.

Given → New This 0.7-percentage-point advantage reversed the order seen at 10% load, where Design A led by 4.5 percentage points.

Limitation However, each design was represented by one prototype with no repeat tests, so this small margin may lie within measurement uncertainty.

Results or Discussion? Results report what was measured; their links are often figure references (As Fig. 1 shows) and comparisons (whereas, compared with). Discussion explains and limits; its links are often summary nouns (this finding), citations (consistent with [3]) and however / therefore. Some theses combine both sections, but a proposed cause always needs evidence.

Activity E · Put the paragraph in order (5 minutes)

E1 · Best order

A. However, only one prototype of each design was tested. B. This 1.2-percentage-point advantage was Design B's largest margin in the tested range. C. At 100% load, Design B achieved 96.3% and Design A 95.1%. Write the best order. Why can B not come first?

E2 · Continue with given → new

In one step-response test, overshoot decreased from 12% to 8%. Write the next sentence. Begin with “This four-percentage-point reduction ...” and add a limitation: how many tests were run?

E3 · Write the topic sentence

Write a topic sentence (12–20 words) to open the reordered paragraph in E1.

Self-check reasoning

E1: C → B → A. B starts with This 1.2-percentage-point advantage, so the reader must first see the numbers in C (96.3 − 95.1 = 1.2). E2: This four-percentage-point reduction was observed in one step-response test; repeatability was not assessed. E3 example: At full load, the tested Design B prototype was more efficient than the Design A prototype.

Model revision · 31–36 minutes

5 · From a weak draft to a thesis paragraph

This page continues the Week 7 revision clinic at paragraph level. The revision keeps every number and every limitation from the draft and removes two unsupported claims. Only the order and the links change.

Before · grammatically plausible, poorly organised

Design B was more efficient. The results were significant. The efficiency of A was 91.0% and B was 86.5% at 10% load. This was different. At 80% load, the respective values were 96.0% and 96.7%. The topology therefore reduced losses. One prototype of each design was tested, with no repeat tests. Losses were not measured. The efficiency of Design B at 100% load was 96.3%, against 95.1% for Design A.

After · revised Results/Discussion paragraph

Topic The two converter designs showed load-dependent efficiency differences. Evidence At 10% load, Design A was more efficient (91.0% versus 86.5%), whereas Design B was slightly more efficient at 80% load (96.7% versus 96.0%) and at 100% load (96.3% versus 95.1%). Reference These comparisons describe the two tested prototypes, not all units built to each design. Limitation Because component losses were not measured, the data cannot show why the patterns differed; because each design was tested once with one prototype, the high-load margins (0.7 and 1.2 percentage points) may lie within measurement uncertainty.

Five revision decisions

Problem in the draftRevisionWhy it matters
“significant” without a reported testDescribe measured percentage-point differencesAvoid unsupported statistical language
“This was different”Use “these comparisons” with a clear antecedentPrecisely connect sentences
Numbers jump between loadsOrder 10% → 80% → 100%Help readers follow conditions
“topology reduced losses”Remove unmeasured mechanismDo not invent a cause
Limitations are separate, unlinked sentencesJoin each limitation to the claim it limitsShows exactly what the data cannot prove

Activity F · Mark the links (3 minutes)

F1 · Find four things

In the revised paragraph, underline (1) the topic phrase, (2) the contrast linking word, (3) the this / these + noun phrase, and (4) the two limitations.

ExtensionF2 · Explain

In one sentence, why is the revised paragraph more coherent than the draft, even though both use the same numbers?

Self-study annotation

Topic: load-dependent efficiency differences. Connector: whereas. Reference: these comparisons. Limitations: component losses were not measured; one prototype per design with no repeat tests. Coherence improves because there is one topic, the evidence is in load order, and each limitation is linked to the claim it limits.

Your own paragraph + peer review · 36–56 minutes

6 · Revise your own research paragraph

Your own writing comes first. Use your two Week 7 sentences or your Week 6 Results/Discussion paragraph. Build one paragraph that links your evidence to its limitation. No draft with you? Use the fallback draft below.

Fallback draft (illustrative): At 14:00, the simulation predicted 4.0 kW. The measured PV mean was 2.8 ± 0.9 kW (n = 5 days). This was lower. The difference was 1.2 kW. Therefore, clouds caused the reduction. Irradiance was not recorded. This was not measured. The reason was uncertain.

Activity G · Write, check, review (20 minutes)

1 · Plan (2 min)Write your main point in one short sentence. Decide: Results, Discussion, or both?
2 · Write (10 min)Write 70–100 words. Check that every it / this / they points to one clear thing.
3 · Check your evidence (3 min)Keep all numbers, units and test conditions. Delete any claim your data cannot prove.

Fallback model (for web self-study only)

At 14:00, the simulated PV output was 4.0 kW, whereas the measured mean was 2.8 ± 0.9 kW (n = 5 days). This 1.2 kW shortfall was larger than the 0.9 kW standard deviation at that time, which was itself three times the deviation at 12:00. However, the available measurements cannot identify the cause, because irradiance was not recorded. Logging irradiance together with output would be required to test whether cloud cover contributed.

Exit ticket + review · 56–60 minutes

7 · Check yourself

Two quick checks. These are formative checks only; no new graded homework or quiz is introduced.

Exit ticket · Four minutes

Exit 1 · Diagnose (2 min)

Remember: cohesion = the links; coherence = one clear message in a logical order.

A paragraph uses however and therefore correctly, but jumps between three unrelated experiments. Is the main problem cohesion or coherence? Explain.

Exit 2 · Revise (2 min)

At 80% load, Design B achieved 96.7% efficiency, compared with 96.0% for Design A. This was better. Rewrite the second sentence with one precise referring noun phrase and cautious wording.
