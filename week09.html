Cohesion & Coherence: From Sentences to Research Paragraphs

Syllabus Week 9: Cohesion and Coherence. Learn reference, transitions, lexical cohesion and logical flow through graduate-level Results/Discussion paragraphs.

Diagnose · 0–5 minutes

0 · Audit the logic of a technical paragraph

In Week 7 you improved the accuracy of individual sentences. Today the question is whether the whole paragraph has one defensible message.

Draft A · linked but misleading

At 80% load, Design B reached 96.7% efficiency and Design A reached 96.0%. Therefore, Design B's topology caused lower power losses. Furthermore, the results prove that Design B is superior in all conditions.

Draft B · coherent and evidence-limited

At 80% load, Design B reached 96.7% efficiency, exceeding Design A by 0.7 percentage points. This comparison favoured Design B at the tested load. However, only one prototype of each design was tested, and no loss breakdown was reported.

Two levels of quality

Cohesion = links readers can see (this comparison, however, repeated technical terms). Coherence = one research point developed through relevant, logically ordered, evidence-supported claims.

Activity A · Five-minute diagnostic

A1 · Evidence audit

Underline the two claims in Draft A that cannot be justified by the stated measurements. Identify the missing evidence for each.

A2 · Link audit

Circle two cohesive expressions in Draft B and identify what each connects.

A3 · Thesis decision

Would Draft B fit Results, Discussion, or both? Give one reason and identify the limitation.

Check the reasoning (self-study)

A1: no measured loss breakdown supports the proposed mechanism; one operating point with one prototype per design cannot prove global superiority. A2: this comparison refers to the 80% load result, and however introduces the sampling/method limitation. A3: it reports a Results comparison and adds a Discussion limitation; the lecturer may divide these moves between sections.

Research reference · 5–13 minutes

1 · Keep every scientific referent clear

Reference tells the reader exactly which result, quantity, component or design you mean. In graduate writing, a vague it or this may change the scientific claim, not merely its style.

Week 7 sentence pair

Design A and Design B were tested at 80% load. It reached 96.7% efficiency.

The antecedent of it is unclear.

Week 9 linked version

Design A and Design B were tested at 80% load. Design B reached 96.7% efficiency.

Repeat the technical referent when ambiguity matters.

Reference decisions for Results/Discussion

DeviceBetter research wordingReason
Repeat exact termDesign B ... Design BProtect the experimental referent
Pronoun after one referentThe sensor was calibrated. It then...Safe only when unambiguous
This + measured nounThis 1.2 kW discrepancy...Makes the referent and scale explicit
This finding / limitationThis single-prototype limitation...Summarises a stated preceding point

Before The simulation predicted 4.0 kW at 14:00; the measured mean was 2.8 kW. This was high.

After The simulation predicted 4.0 kW at 14:00; the measured mean was 2.8 kW. This 1.2 kW prediction–measurement gap requires further investigation.

Activity B · Repair research references (8 minutes)

B1 · Disambiguate the design

At 80% load, Design A and Design B achieved 96.0% and 96.7% efficiency, respectively. It was higher. Rewrite the last sentence with the correct referent and quantified difference.

B2 · Replace bare this

At 14:00, the predicted output exceeded the measured mean by 1.2 kW. This requires investigation. Rewrite with a specific noun phrase and no causal claim.

B3 · Sampling reference

One prototype of each converter design was evaluated. They cannot establish between-unit variability. Make the sampling limitation the subject.

Self-check models

B1: Design B was 0.7 percentage points more efficient than Design A at 80% load. B2: This 1.2 kW prediction–measurement difference requires investigation. B3: Evaluating one prototype per design does not establish between-unit variability.

Discourse relations · 13–22 minutes

2 · Connect sentences without overstating evidence

Week 7 taught coordination and subordination within a sentence. Week 9 uses those choices to guide the reader between sentences and across an entire Results/Discussion paragraph.

Move → relationship → wording

Research moveLogical relationEvidence-safe model
Results: comparecontrast · whereas / howeverA led at 10% load; however, B led at 80% load.
Results: add measurementaddition · in additionVoltage was logged. In addition, current was measured.
Discussion: infer cautiouslyqualified consequenceOnly one prototype was tested; therefore, between-unit variability was not assessed.
Discussion: limit inferenceconcession / limitationB was higher at 80% load. However, the sample comprised one prototype per design.
Connector discipline: however expresses contrast; therefore needs a valid inference. Write “Sentence 1; however, sentence 2” or “Sentence 1. However, sentence 2.” Never replace scientific justification with a linking word.

Activity C · Defend the relationship (9 minutes)

C1 · Choose and justify

Design A led at 10% load. ______, Design B led at 80% load. Choose However / Therefore and name the relationship.

C2 · Evaluate “therefore”

Only one prototype per design was evaluated; therefore, the sample cannot establish between-unit variability. Is this inference defensible? Explain.

C3 · Repair a causal claim

“The predicted PV output was 4.0 kW; therefore, the observed mean was 2.8 kW.” Replace the link and explain why.

C4 · Punctuate a research transition

The efficiency difference was 0.7 percentage points at 80% load however no repeat tests were reported. Rewrite using a semicolon and a comma.

Self-check reasoning

C1: However, because the stronger design changes with operating load. C2: Yes; a single tested unit of each design cannot show between-unit variability. C3: Use whereas / but / however; the prediction does not cause the measured value. C4: The efficiency difference was 0.7 percentage points at 80% load; however, no repeat tests were reported.

Technical noun chains · 22–30 minutes

3 · Maintain precise terminology across sentences

Lexical cohesion is controlled use of repeated and related technical terms. In a thesis, variety is useful only when it does not silently change the measured variable, component or method.

Design B→this converter design→its measured efficiency→the 0.7-point advantage

Safe chain

Design B achieved 96.7% efficiency at 80% load. This measured efficiency was 0.7 percentage points higher than the value for Design A.

Unsafe substitution

The inverter was evaluated. The generator produced the same waveform.

Different engineering terms may denote different devices.

Track the research noun, not synonyms

Starting termPossible chainUnsafe shift
measurementthe measured mean → this observed valuesimulation as though it were measured
predictionthe simulated output → that predictionobserved output without distinction
prototypethis tested unit → the single prototypeall manufactured units
differencethe 1.2 kW gap → this discrepancya statistically significant difference (untested)

Activity D · Build an accurate chain (8 minutes)

D1 · Map the referent

In “The predicted output was 4.0 kW. The measured mean was 2.8 kW. This discrepancy was 1.2 kW,” what exactly is this discrepancy?

D2 · Repair terminology

Rewrite “Design B was tested at 80% load. The generator reached 96.7% efficiency.” without substituting an unmentioned device.

D3 · Link without overclaiming

Join these facts in two sentences: Design B = 96.7% at 80% load; Design B = 96.3% at 100% load; only one prototype was tested. Use at least one precise reference.

Self-check models

D1: the 1.2 kW difference between the simulated 4.0 kW output and measured 2.8 kW mean. D2: Design B was tested at 80% load and reached 96.7% efficiency. The referent is Design B; do not introduce an unmentioned generator. D3: Design B reached 96.7% efficiency at 80% load and 96.3% at 100% load. These values were obtained from a single prototype of that design.

Given → new · 30–39 minutes

4 · Build a coherent research paragraph

Given → new is an information-flow strategy: begin from an idea the reader can identify, then add the next relevant finding. At paragraph level, choose a purposeful route rather than a random collection of well-written sentences.

1 · PointAnnounce the research comparison.
2 · EvidenceReport quantities and conditions.
3 · InterpretationExplain only what follows from data.
4 · LimitationState what remains untested.

Worked chain: each sentence earns the next

Point + Evidence At 80% load, Design B achieved 96.7% efficiency, compared with 96.0% for Design A.

Given → New This 0.7-percentage-point advantage favoured Design B under the tested condition.

Limitation However, this comparison involved one prototype of each design, so between-unit variability could not be evaluated.

Results vs Discussion: Values and tested conditions belong in Results; qualifications and interpretations often belong in Discussion. They may be combined when the dissertation structure allows, but a proposed cause must be supported.

Activity E · Reconstruct a research paragraph (9 minutes)

E1 · Order by dependency

A. However, only one prototype of each design was tested. B. This 1.2-percentage-point advantage favoured Design B. C. At 100% load, Design B achieved 96.3% and Design A 95.1%. Write the best order and explain why B cannot lead.

E2 · Carry a measured result forward

In one step-response test, overshoot decreased from 12% to 8%. Write the next sentence beginning “This four-percentage-point reduction...” and qualify its repeatability.

E3 · Choose the topic

Propose a precise 4–8-word heading for the reordered converter paragraph.

Self-check reasoning

E1: C → B → A; B depends on the earlier numerical comparison. E2: This four-percentage-point reduction was observed in one step-response test; repeatability was not assessed. E3 example: Full-load efficiency comparison of converter designs.

Expert model · 39–46 minutes

5 · From a Week 7 draft to a thesis paragraph

Model revision: this is the paragraph-level continuation of Week 7’s sentence-revision clinic. Every number and limitation is retained; the scientific relationships become easier to follow.

Before · grammatically plausible, poorly organised

Design B was more efficient. The results were significant. The efficiency of A was 91.0% and B was 86.5% at 10% load. This was different. At 80% load, the respective values were 96.0% and 96.7%. The topology therefore reduced losses. One prototype of each design was tested. The efficiency of Design B at 100% load was 96.3%.

After · revised Results/Discussion paragraph

Topic The two converter designs showed load-dependent efficiency differences. Evidence At 10% load, Design A was more efficient (91.0% versus 86.5%), whereas Design B was slightly more efficient at 80% load (96.7% versus 96.0%) and at 100% load (96.3% versus 95.1%). Reference These comparisons describe the tested prototypes, not the variability of a wider population. Limitation Because only one prototype of each design was assessed and component losses were not separately measured, the data cannot establish why the efficiency patterns differed.

Five postgraduate revision decisions

Problem in first draftRevisionWhy it matters
“significant” without a reported testDescribe measured percentage-point differencesAvoid unsupported statistical language
“This was different”Use “these comparisons” with a clear antecedentPrecisely connect sentences
Numbers jump between loadsOrder 10% → 80% → 100%Help readers follow conditions
“topology reduced losses”Remove unmeasured mechanismDo not invent a cause
Limitation is detachedConnect single-prototype limit to inferenceCalibrate generalisability

Activity F · Annotate the revision (7 minutes)

F1 · Trace

Underline the topic phrase, the comparison connector, the referring noun phrase, and the sampling limitation in the revised paragraph.

F2 · Explain

In one sentence, why is the revised paragraph more coherent than the first, even though both contain technical terms?

Self-study annotation

Topic: load-dependent efficiency differences. Connector: whereas. Reference: these comparisons. Limitation: one prototype of each design and no separately measured component losses. Coherence improves through one topic, ordered evidence and calibrated claims.

Independent revision + peer check · 46–57 minutes

6 · Revise your Week 7 research paragraph

Your own research writing comes first. Use your Week 7 revised sentences or your Week 6 Results/Discussion paragraph. Extend them into one focused paragraph with an explicit connection between evidence and its limitation. If your draft is unavailable, use the fallback below.

Fallback draft (illustrative): At 14:00, the simulation predicted 4.0 kW. The measured PV mean was 2.8 kW. This was lower. The difference was 1.2 kW. Therefore, clouds caused the reduction. Irradiance was not recorded. This was not measured. The reason was uncertain.

Activity G · Authentic revision and peer review (11 minutes)

1 · Research point (2 min)Name the main measurable finding and choose Results or Discussion emphasis.
2 · Rebuild (5 min)Write 70–100 words; give every link an identifiable referent.
3 · Evidence audit (2 min)Preserve units and conditions; remove claims beyond the dataset.
4 · Peer review (2 min)Explain why each logical link and paragraph move is justified.
Your revised paragraph (aim 70–100 words; accurate meaning matters more than exact length)

Peer-review criteria: one research point; precise measurements; logical sequence; clear this + noun references; justified transitions; explicit sampling or measurement limitation. No new graded assignment is created.
Fallback model (for web self-study only)

At 14:00, the simulated PV output was 4.0 kW, whereas the measured mean was 2.8 kW. This 1.2 kW discrepancy indicates that the prediction exceeded the observation under the reported condition. However, the available measurements do not identify the cause of the difference because irradiance was not recorded. Additional measurements under comparable conditions would be required before the influence of solar conditions could be evaluated.

Exit & independent reference · 57–60 minutes

7 · Check the research argument, not the connectors

Check whether you can transfer sentence control into paragraph-level academic writing. These are formative checks only; no new graded homework or quiz is introduced.

Exit ticket · Three minutes

Exit 1 · Diagnose (1 min)

A paragraph uses however and therefore correctly, but jumps among three unrelated experiments. Is the main issue cohesion or coherence? Explain.

Exit 2 · Revise (2 min)

At 80% load, Design B achieved 96.7% efficiency, compared with 96.0% for Design A. This was better. Rewrite the second sentence with one precise referring noun phrase and cautious wording.
